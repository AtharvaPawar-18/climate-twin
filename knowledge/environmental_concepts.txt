Temperature describes the measured environmental air temperature.

Relative humidity describes the amount of moisture in the air relative to the amount the air can hold at that temperature.

Heat index is an indicator that combines temperature and humidity to represent how hot conditions may feel.

Wet-bulb temperature is the lowest temperature that can be reached through evaporative cooling under the measured conditions. It is useful when considering the interaction between temperature and humidity.

Wet-bulb globe temperature is an environmental heat-stress measurement that accounts for temperature, humidity, radiant heat, and wind effects depending on the measurement method.

Wind speed describes the speed of moving air.

Wind direction describes the direction from which the wind is measured.

Environmental anomalies are observations that differ substantially from the patterns learned or defined by the ClimateTwin prototype.

An environmental anomaly does not automatically mean a dangerous weather event. It means that the observation is unusual relative to the model or baseline used by the system.

Short-term environmental forecasting attempts to estimate future environmental variables from recent observations and temporal patterns.
